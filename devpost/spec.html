Handful Is Not a Unit

How the app is built: the pieces, where each one lives, and how mom's words travel from the kitchen to the recipe card.

Status: approved · 2026-10-05

The one rule

The AI reads, the app measures. Gemini only reports "mom said a handful of flour". The amount for "a handful" comes from a fixed table inside the app. The same words always give the same estimate, it is always labeled, and the daughter can correct it. When the table has no entry, the app asks a question instead of guessing.

Where it can break, and what happens instead

The built-in ears stop or mishear

The sign shows "not listening", listening restarts by itself, and the type-or-paste box is always there. If the three-times kitchen test fails, the speech service fallback is built, using the same Gemini key.

The microphone is not allowed

A plain message explains how to allow it in Safari. Typing and pasting still work.

Gemini is slow or unreachable

The text is kept and retried once. If it still fails, a small "couldn't understand that, try again" message appears. No line is invented.

A rough word the table does not know

A question, never a guess.

Photos fill the iPhone or iPad's storage

Photos are shrunk before saving. If one cannot be saved, the recipe is saved without it and the daughter is told.

Agreed at review: the recommendations

  • Gemini as the one AI service. Gemini is recommended because it can also take audio, so the speech fallback needs no second service. Tradeoff: mom's words go to Google.
  • The paid Gemini tier. On the free tier, what is sent may be used to improve Google's products. With a child in the kitchen, paid is the safer choice. A recipe should cost well under a dollar.
  • Next.js and TypeScript as the way the app is built.
  • The fixed measuring table.
  • Drawn food-group icons as the ingredient pictures.
